\# Phishing Analysis Basics



\## Overview



Phishing is a social engineering attack where attackers attempt to trick users into revealing sensitive information or executing malicious actions.



Common phishing goals:

\- stealing credentials

\- delivering malware

\- financial fraud

\- account compromise



Phishing is one of the most common cyber attack techniques used against organizations.



\---



\## Types of Phishing Attacks



\### Email Phishing



Attackers send fraudulent emails pretending to be legitimate organizations.



Examples:

\- fake Microsoft login pages

\- banking scams

\- password reset emails



\---



\### Spear Phishing



Targeted phishing attacks aimed at specific individuals or departments.



Examples:

\- finance teams

\- executives

\- administrators



\---



\### Whaling



Phishing attacks specifically targeting senior executives or high-value individuals.



\---



\### Smishing



Phishing through SMS or text messages.



\---



\### Vishing



Voice-based phishing using phone calls.



\---



\## Common Phishing Indicators



Examples:

\- suspicious sender addresses

\- urgent language

\- fake login links

\- spelling mistakes

\- unexpected attachments



\---



\## Malicious Attachments



Attackers may send:

\- malicious Office documents

\- PDFs

\- ZIP files

\- executable malware



These attachments may install malware when opened.



\---



\## Credential Harvesting



Attackers create fake login pages to steal:

\- usernames

\- passwords

\- MFA codes



Victims unknowingly submit credentials to attackers.



\---



\## Business Email Compromise (BEC)



BEC attacks involve impersonating trusted individuals to:

\- request payments

\- steal data

\- manipulate employees



BEC attacks can cause major financial losses.



\---



\## URL Analysis



Security analysts inspect:

\- suspicious domains

\- shortened URLs

\- lookalike domains

\- hidden redirects



Example:

```text

micr0soft-login.com

```



\---



\## Email Header Analysis



Headers help identify:

\- sender IP addresses

\- mail servers

\- spoofed domains

\- suspicious routing



\---



\## Phishing in SOC Operations



SOC analysts investigate:

\- suspicious emails

\- malicious attachments

\- credential theft attempts

\- phishing campaigns



\---



\## Threat Intelligence and Phishing



Security teams use:

\- IOC feeds

\- malicious domain lists

\- phishing indicators



to improve detection.



\---



\## Prevention Techniques



Recommended practices:

\- security awareness training

\- MFA enforcement

\- email filtering

\- attachment sandboxing

\- domain monitoring



\---



\## Common Security Tools



Examples:

\- Microsoft Defender

\- Proofpoint

\- Mimecast

\- VirusTotal



\---



\## Importance in Cybersecurity



Phishing remains one of the most effective attack methods because it targets human behavior instead of technical vulnerabilities.



\---



\## Conclusion



Phishing analysis helps organizations identify malicious emails, prevent credential theft, and reduce social engineering risks. Continuous monitoring and user awareness are essential for defending against phishing attacks.
